# ERIS MORN — DEEP-BEARER LENS CANDIDATE

**STATUS:** `NOT YET PLACED / ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT`

**Critical continuity note:** Eris Morn was **never previously added to the established Deep-Bearer Inclusion/Exclusion Lens.** Do not search for or reconstruct a nonexistent canonical placement. Any eventual position must be deliberately workshopped and approved before becoming canon.

## Why Eris Matters

Eris is unusually relevant to the Deep-Bearer philosophy because her entire history revolves around **survival after catastrophic subtraction**.

She entered the Hellmouth as a Guardian with a fireteam.

She emerged without that fireteam, without her Ghost, without the ordinary relationship to the Light she entered with, and physically altered by what she endured.

That makes her immediately interesting through the Resonance distinction between:

**What Was** — Eris the Guardian who descended with Eriana-3, Toland, Sai Mota, Vell Tarlowe, and Omar Agah.

**What Is** — the Eris who survived the Hellmouth and continued afterward.

The loss of What Was did not make What Was **Never Was**, nor did it prevent a new What Is from existing.

That parallels Deep-Bearer philosophy without automatically making Eris a Deep-Bearer.

## Eris and Drowning

Do **not** casually state that Eris has Drowned in the Resonance-specific sense.

She has undergone experiences that make her an exceptionally strong candidate for understanding the *idea* of Drowning: isolation, loss, transformation, survival, grief, Darkness, Hive knowledge, and continuation after the destruction of a former life.

But:

> **Understanding something analogous to Drowning is not the same as having Drowned.**

This is the same kind of categorical discipline used with Rhulk: proximity to the concept does not grant the designation.

## Eris and What Was

Eris has one of the strongest relationships in Destiny with persistent memory.

Her dead fireteam remains foundational to who she becomes afterward. Their deaths are not erased by her survival, and her continued existence does not restore them.

That makes her potentially receptive to:

> **The Deep Persists What Was.**

But she would likely understand immediately that persistence does **not** mean restoration.

What Was remaining causally true does not mean the dead are secretly alive, recoverable, or waiting inside the Deep.

That distinction is particularly important for Eris because of her history with grief and the Nightmares of the Moon.

## Eris and What Is

Eris also embodies an important Resonance principle:

> **What Was being stripped away does not necessarily destroy What Is.**

She did not return from the Hellmouth unchanged.

She also did not cease to exist because the Guardian she had been could no longer exist in the same form.

That gives her conceptual overlap with **Crow**, although their circumstances are radically different.

Crow's continuity problem centers on an identity whose What Was was initially inaccessible.

Eris remembers hers.

She carries it.

That distinction should matter if/when her Lens position is developed.

## Eris and the Deep

Eris possesses extensive experience with Hive metaphysics, Darkness, trauma, memory, and transformation.

However, Resonance must preserve one foundational rule:

> **The Deep has no agency. It simply is.**

Therefore Eris's knowledge of Hive interpretations of “the Deep,” Sword Logic, Darkness, or the Witness does not automatically mean she already understands the Resonance project's Deep correctly.

Her potential importance is precisely that she possesses enough adjacent knowledge to recognize that the Deep-Bearer's explanation may require **separating the underlying phenomenon from the ideologies that have claimed to explain it.**

That could make encountering a genuine Deep-Bearer particularly significant for her.

## Eris and the Witness

Eris has extensive reason to distrust cosmologies presented as absolute truth by entities associated with Darkness.

A Deep-Bearer claiming:

> **The Deep does not want.**
>
> **The Deep does not judge.**
>
> **The Deep does not choose.**
>
> **The Deep Persists.**

would therefore present Eris with a potentially important distinction:

The Deep-Bearer is not offering another ideology demanding obedience.

They're describing a causal condition.

Whether Eris accepts that distinction—and how quickly—is **not yet canonically decided.**

## Eris and Nightmares

This is potentially one of the richest areas for a future workshop.

Nightmares manifest memory, grief, trauma, and guilt in ways that superficially resemble Resonance's concern with **What Was**.

But they must not be conflated.

A Nightmare is not automatically an Echo.

An Echo is not automatically a Nightmare.

And persistence of What Was does not require psychological suffering.

Eris's intimate experience with Nightmares could therefore make her particularly sensitive to the distinction between:

> **Something exploiting your memory**

and

> **the causal truth that the remembered event actually occurred.**

That distinction deserves deliberate development rather than inference.

## Eris and the Perpetual

Potentially relevant, but **UNRESOLVED**.

Given Eris's relationship with loss and persistent identity, the Perpetual would likely be deeply significant to her.

However, do not invent an Eris-specific application of the Perpetual merely because one seems plausible.

The Savathûn/Perpetual connection is established.

An Eris/Perpetual interpretation is not.
