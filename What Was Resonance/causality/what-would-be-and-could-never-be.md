# What Would Be and Could Never Be

**Status: CURRENT AUTHOR-SUPPLIED CONCEPTUAL / WORKSHOP STATE.** The core relationship is now supplied explicitly by [S09](../sources/2026-09-23-resonance-corrective-directive.md). Complete formal mechanics and the full underlying live-workshop exchange have not been recovered.

What Would Be can become Could Never Be when a once-valid causal branch becomes inaccessible. The fact that the branch was once possible remains What Was, but that branch cannot be actualized from the new causal state.

This distinction is relative to the changed causal state described by the author. It does not erase the branch's former possibility or prove that the unrealized outcome actually happened. The fact of prior possibility is What Was; an outcome that never occurred is not thereby made a persisted event. **The Deep Persists What Was, not What Never Was.**

The [Laws of Causality](../books-of-sorrow/laws-of-causality/2026-09-09/README.md), section 2, distinguishes a failed path from knowledge that an outcome can never occur. Failure alone is therefore not the missing condition. S09 supplies the current conceptual condition: inaccessibility from the new causal state.
